# Pocket Drums course design

This course begins with an established backbeat, sixteenth-note counting and alternating strokes as prerequisites. Twelve original four-bar studies progress from controlling a single surface to maintaining time through a full-kit phrase. Starting tempos give the player room to diagnose a problem. Difficulty comes from what the limbs must do independently and how the phrase resolves.

| Teaching source | Structure used in Pocket Drums |
|---|---|
| [Ted Reed, *Progressive Steps to Syncopation*](https://www.alfred.com/products/progressive-steps-to-syncopation-for-the-modern-drummer-00-17308) | Reed’s vocabulary includes accented rhythms, triplets and sixteenths. The reading chapter separates accented attacks, low taps and rests before moving a syncopated line to the kick. |
| [Gary Chester, *The New Breed*](https://www.halleonard.com/product/6620100/the-new-breed) | Chester emphasizes reading, coordination, lead-hand independence and awareness of the click. The kick-reader study keeps the hand pattern constant while the foot reads a changing line. Voice-specific guides let the player hear each part before combining the limbs. |
| [Gary Chaffee, *Time Functioning Patterns*](https://www.alfred.com/products/patterns-time-functioning-patterns-00-el03586cd) | Chaffee groups work on cymbal ostinatos, independence and linear phrasing. The kit chapter contrasts a layered half-time shuffle, a linear relay with exactly one attack per slot, and orchestrated doubles over a repeating foot pattern. |
| [Berklee Online, *Drum Set Performance 101*](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/drum-set-performance-101) | The syllabus connects coordination, subdivision and ghost notes with performance. The final chapter assesses recoveries within the groove, including a fill that crosses beat one and returns to the backbeat on two. |
| [Percussive Arts Society rudiment directory](https://pas.org/rudiments/) | Common sticking vocabulary connects pad work to kit application. The trainer allows the accent to move independently of the sticking and lead hand. |

## Practice sequence

1. Hear the complete four-bar example and count the quarter note.
2. Isolate the bar or transition named in the lesson. In a multi-voice study, select the limb or surface that is drifting. The snare guide includes its ghost notes.
3. Use the lesson’s correction, then play the adjacent pair to check the entry and exit.
4. Restore the full phrase and mute the guide. Judge the written listening checkpoint before logging a clean take.

The pad route contains four complete hand studies. The kit route adds eight coordination and performance studies. Four-bar goals and two-bar legacy records remain separate. Linked commercial-song transcriptions open at the provider. The original scores use the methods’ teaching ideas and contain no copied book pages or course transcriptions.
