# Designing bass and drum practice for Fretlab

The expansion serves a guitarist learning either side of a rhythm section. Six original two-bar arrangements pair a playable bass line with a drum part. A player can hear the arrangement, isolate either part, then remove the part they intend to play. The same study can be opened from either instrument.

## What the teaching sources establish

**Bass articulation.** Andrew Pouska describes open-string resonance as a rhythmic problem as well as a technical one. A string left sounding can mask the attack that follows it. His muting lesson assigns complementary work to the two hands. The opening study keeps pitch and drum pattern constant while halving the bass notes’ duration. A learner can listen for the release, point to it on the grid and reproduce it. [StudyBass, Open-String Muting](https://www.studybass.com/lessons/bass-technique/open-string-muting/)

**Note choice.** Pouska’s common-pattern curriculum moves from roots to octaves, fifths and approaches. These relationships give a guitarist a way into writing a supportive bass line. The fifths exercise changes harmony from G to C while preserving the interval shape. The octave exercise crosses a silent string. The approach exercise resolves C and C♯ to D across the repeat. [StudyBass, Common Bass Patterns](https://www.studybass.com/study-guide/studybass-fundamentals-one/common-bass-patterns/)

**Ensemble listening.** Yamaha’s bass lesson connects pocket to timing, silence, dynamics and note duration, with the kick as a starting reference. In the offbeat study, both instruments catch the “and” of two, then the bass answers in space left by the kick. Hearing both parts first makes that departure audible. Removing the bass makes the drummer’s kick a reference the learner can actually use. [Yamaha, Getting Your Bass Into the Groove](https://hub.yamaha.com/guitars/bass/getting-your-bass-into-the-groove/)

**Kit coordination.** Zildjian’s method introduces the hands, foot, eighth-note timekeeping, fills and syncopated bass drum in stages. Its groove library demonstrates a pattern at multiple speeds and supplies backing. Fretlab starts with quarter-note hats, holds the backbeat steady through the first offbeat kick and gives each study an adjustable tempo. The count-in establishes the pulse before the first simultaneous attack. [Zildjian, Groove Library](https://ae.zildjian.com/education/beginner-drum-set-lessons/groove-library-01/)

**Dynamics and sticking.** Drumeo teaches a low, quiet snare stroke between stronger backbeats. PAS describes sticking as the hand sequence used to play a passage. The ghost study gives soft taps a separate audible level and parenthesized mark. The final study frees the hat hand on beat four for four alternating snare singles. A clean fill includes the kick and hat on the next downbeat. [Drumeo, Five Beginner Drum Techniques](https://www.drumeo.com/beat/best-beginner-drum-book-5-lessons/) · [Percussive Arts Society, An Analytical Approach to Rudiments](https://pas.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ECV0720-23.pdf)

## What to listen for

| Study | Bass checkpoint | Drum checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Leave room for the snare | Bar two leaves more silence than bar one | Hat and kick or snare start together |
| Roots under a changing riff | The G–D shape becomes C–G cleanly | Hat eighths stay even through the harmony change |
| Catch the offbeat kick | Bass and kick coincide on the “and” of two | Extra foot attacks leave the hand pattern steady |
| Octaves over a half-time beat | Only the intended string rings | One snare on beat three preserves the four-beat pulse |
| A quiet answer to the backbeat | The approach resolves on the first beat of the loop | Ghost taps stay audible beneath the backbeats |
| A fill that brings the riff back | Bass releases its final D into the fill | Four alternating snare strokes lead back to one |

## Product decisions and limits

Four-string E standard covers full-scale and short-scale bass, including a Bronco. A kick, snare and closed hi-hat are enough for every drum part. Eight cells per table keep each subdivision aligned to its label on a phone. Holds occupy real cells and the player schedules the same durations. The metronome counts quarters throughout, including when the bass groups eight eighth notes as 3+3+2.

Tempo presets provide starting points. Players decide when a take is clean and log their own best tempo for each part. These sources inform the sequence and musical checkpoints. They do not validate a particular starting BPM or measure learning outcomes for this implementation.

## How books, courses and apps shaped the individual paths

| Instrument and reference | Teaching decision in the source | Application in Pocket Rehearsal |
|---|---|---|
| Bass · Ed Friedland, *Hal Leonard Bass Method Book 1* | The contents introduce open strings and low-position notes before expanding rhythmic vocabulary. The book pairs instruction with demonstration and play-along audio. | Start with open E and A. The register and chord-quality studies stay within the first five frets. [Publisher’s book reader](https://reader.halleonard.com/viewbook_v2/Hal-Leonard-Bass-Method-Book-1?isml=1) |
| Bass · BassBuzz, *Beginner to Badass* | The public syllabus names alternating plucks, string crossings, rhythm and tablature as specific skills. | The two-finger study fixes the pitch within each bar. The player can hear unequal attacks before adding a string change. [Course outline](https://www.bassbuzz.com/beginner-to-badass/lessons-overview) |
| Bass · Yousician | Its open bass path lets a player choose a technical or musical theme and revisit it. | Keep the lessons ordered and freely selectable. Changing the third of A gives the theory choice a consequence the player can hear. [Bass learning path](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005190525-How-the-bass-learning-path-works) |
| Drums · George Lawrence Stone, *Stick Control* | The publisher emphasizes hand coordination, touch and weaker-hand development through stroke combinations. | Display sticking for both lead hands. Hold the sounded rhythm constant while changing from singles to doubles. [Publisher description](https://www.alfred.com/products/stick-control-00-32749) |
| Drums · Ted Reed, *Syncopation and Rolls for the Drum Set* | The publisher describes alternating singles and doubles across different tempos alongside syncopation work. | Teach the second stroke of a double independently, then move a kick against steady hands in a separate coordination lesson. [Publisher description](https://www.alfred.com/products/syncopation-and-rolls-for-the-drum-set-00-17311) |
| Drums · Melodics | Practice Mode allows slower tempos and looped passages. | Two short bars repeat at a chosen BPM. Turning off the guide leaves the count audible for an acoustic kit or pad. [Practice Mode](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/6777027-practice-mode) |

The five bass lessons progress from muting to alternate plucking, register, chord quality and a pedal-note riff. The five drum lessons progress from balanced singles to doubles, accents, foot independence and moving a sticking around the kit. Guitar retains its nine-unit fretboard path and existing workouts. The six ensemble arrangements are the point where bass and drums meet. A bassist can study major versus minor without a drum lesson, and a drummer can work on touch with just a pad.

Method comparisons used public contents, publisher descriptions, course outlines and support documentation. The exercises, arrangements and audio are original. This review did not evaluate paid course interiors or test learner outcomes.
