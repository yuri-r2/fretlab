# Advanced practice direction

Research and app audit, 4 November 2026. Primary audience: an experienced alternative, Midwest emo and shoegaze guitarist who already knows common open chords, barre chords and scale boxes.

## What needed to change

The app had useful transport controls and a substantial library, but its default curriculum asked an experienced player to repeat familiar material: subdivisions, note maps, pentatonic boxes and basic chord shapes. The chord catalog answered “How do I finger this chord?” more often than “What new sound could I make with it?” Its progress model also emphasized tempo, while a good atmospheric part may depend on sustain, voice balance, space or an intentional dissonance.

The design direction is discovery followed by application. Each featured idea needs an audible character, a small technical problem, a musical variation and an original phrase. Harmonic complexity alone is not an engaging curriculum: an unusual symbol without a clear sound or use is another memorization task.

## What fits the chosen styles

**Open strings should do musical work.** A fixed E or B can become a third, fifth, major seventh or 11th as the harmony moves. The interesting lesson is hearing that change while keeping a voice connected. Close seconds, minor/major-seventh tension and Lydian sharp-11 colors offer useful contrast without requiring a dense six-note chord every time.

**Think in layers.** Bass, inner harmony and top melody can move independently. In [Yvette Young's original Reverb demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GKdsaRU2u8g), open tunings are a starting point for writing with two-handed guitar textures. Her [artist interview](https://guitar.com/features/interviews/yvette-young-covet-catharsis/) discusses singing melodies and lyrical phrasing. These are examples of an artist's process, not evidence that one tuning or technique is universally best. They support a melody-first design and an optional alternate-tuning branch.

**Make rhythm a compositional choice.** A three-note cell repeated through 4/4, a displaced entrance, an asymmetric bar, or space completed by delay can make familiar pitches feel new. The user should be able to hear the beat separately from the accent pattern. A challenge should specify the phrase length and where it returns to beat 1.

**Effects should reveal a good part.** Study the rubbing notes and voice balance clean before adding reverb or glide. The app's synthesized guide is a timing and pitch model, not a faithful shoegaze production or an imitation of a particular guitarist.

## App patterns worth adapting

[Pickup Music's documented homepage](https://help.pickupmusic.com/en/articles/10513850-navigating-the-homepage) distinguishes core pathways, short challenges, artist material, workouts and jams. Its guided lessons culminate in musical application. The useful pattern for Fretlab is a short journey from a sound to a performance, with foundations available separately. This documentation describes the product; it does not establish comparative learning effectiveness or prove that users prefer every feature.

[Pickup's neo-soul course](https://www.pickupmusic.com/guitar/guitar-classes/get-the-neo-soul-sound-in-10-days) is another example of a focused stylistic outcome rather than an undifferentiated library. [Its Plini instructor page](https://www.pickupmusic.com/guitar/guitar-instructors/plini) describes a structured approach to melodic prog solo writing. Fretlab can use that focused structure while keeping alternative, emo and shoegaze as its primary direction.

[TrueFire's rock path](https://truefire.com/learning-paths/rock-guitar-learning-path/a9) combines voicing vocabulary, creative rhythm and genre application at intermediate and advanced levels. Fretlab can connect those dimensions within one practice study instead of asking players to browse unrelated drills. Familiar transport patterns also matter: the playback tools described with Peckham's book include loop points, key changes and speed control. Fretlab already has these; the remaining interaction problem was giving enough warning before a key transition.

## What the learning evidence can and cannot say

A [2024 contextual-interference meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122/full) favored random practice overall, but its applied-setting effect was small and statistically non-significant. These were varied motor tasks, not a trial of advanced guitar apps.

A [study of 20 advanced violinists](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00224294231222801) found blocked practice performed best on its 24-hour retention test. It does not imply that all musical practice should be blocked. Together, these sources argue against automatically making every repetition unpredictable. Let a player stabilize a difficult phrase, then deliberately vary its key, rhythm or context. Key cycling remains optional and follows a predictable circle of fourths.

The app's spaced reviews and first-attempt checks remain useful design choices, but no study here proves that Fretlab's exact intervals or two-clean-runs rule are optimal. Ratings are self-reported. A skip is a preference about recommendations, not evidence of mastery.

## Implemented direction

- **24 discovery voicings:** 12 fixed open-string grips and 12 fully fretted movable forms. Eight from either group appear first. Extensions, clusters, fourth stacks, musical-use cues and explicit omitted tones replace the basic open/barre inventory. Open and movable groups remain mutually exclusive.
- **Five original four-step studies:** open-string changing harmony; one moving inner voice; motifs through changes; three-note cells across 4/4; stacked fourths over a drone. The open-string study ends with a written technical challenge combining pinches, sixteenth-note string skips and 3–3–2 accents. The other four end in constrained creative tasks. Existing advanced and alternate-tuning material joins these in a 12-study featured selection.
- **Daily practice follows a sound:** hear/map the chosen study, isolate its challenge, then apply it. Fresh sessions do not automatically insert a basic note-name lesson. The fundamentals remain available in Learn and exercise search.
- **Specific success criteria:** sustain a common tone, expose an inner melody, preserve a motif and its rests, or keep 4/4 under displaced accents. Creative tasks use a comfortable tempo and ask for an intentional phrase.
- **Reversible skipping:** “Too easy — skip” removes a study from automatic recommendations and reviews without changing completion, best tempo or history. It remains searchable and can be restored in the player.
- **Return to the session:** an explicit Open block action in the persistent session bar reopens the active exercise with its step, key, tempo and position while retaining the block timer.
- **Preparation before key changes:** the next key, tempo and opening frets are visible early; the scrolling tab contains the approaching key's actual notes. Previous-key notes remain visible across the boundary. Clicking upcoming notation prepares that key at the selected note. Audio scheduling and the preview share the same next-loop plan.
- **High-contrast Inked appearance:** near-white/near-black backgrounds, teal accents, clear boundaries and individual marker-label contrast. [Figma's neutral schemes](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/website-color-schemes/) inform the direction; [WCAG enhanced contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html) supplies a 7:1 text target.

## Further work to validate with playing

1. Try each featured study on a real guitar, at a comfortable tempo, with a clean sound and then the intended effects. Pitch/fingering checks cannot assess hand comfort, taste or production quality.
2. On the following day, play a phrase from memory and move the idea to another key or progression. Returning to a saved BPM alone is not evidence that it transfers.
3. Ask whether a voicing led to a usable part. Replace shapes that are merely unusual with examples that players actually return to.
4. Test whether the next-key warning gives enough preparation at high tempos. A longer optional transition/count-in may suit some players, but adding silence to every loop would change the continuity of the exercise.
5. Consider phrase recall by ear, upper-voice melody paths and richer accompaniment next. These are proposed extensions, not shipped capabilities. Human musical feedback remains a separate need; the app does not listen to or grade the guitar.
