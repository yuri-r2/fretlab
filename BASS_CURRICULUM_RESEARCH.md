# How Pocket Bass sequences practice

The thirteen lessons use the early sequence of Ed Friedland’s *Hal Leonard Bass Method* as their starting point. First learn what the open string sounds like, start and end it deliberately, then find a nearby fretted pitch. Later units follow StudyBass’s move from roots and octaves to choosing notes that explain a chord change. The final chapter combines those skills in original two-bar performance studies. This is a compact electric-bass course for riff writers. Standard notation reading, slap, and extended song-form study would require further material.

## What the courses actually teach

Isolate the alternating stroke before crossing strings, then use an E-standard arrangement of “Come As You Are” to apply string crossing in a repeating part. |
| [StudyBass · Common Bass Patterns](https://www.studybass.com/study-guide/studybass-fundamentals-one/common-bass-patterns/) | The progression goes from roots through octaves and fifths to characteristic added tones and chromatic approaches. | Find C in two registers. Compare A major and minor over the same root and fifth. Carry one chord tone into the next harmony. Finally, approach a target from a semitone away and then enclose it from both sides. |
| [Berklee Online · Bass Performance 101 syllabus](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/bass-performance-101) | The syllabus connects muting and pitch work to triads, subdivision, groove, harmony and ostinatos, with repertoire examples. | An open-E pedal anchors a changing upper voice. In the advanced burst exercise, the same pedal exposes rushing immediately after a four-note group. The chord-tone tool lets the learner hear the third change under a fixed root. |
| [Scott’s Bass Lessons · Players Path](https://scottsbasslessons.com/players-path) | Learners enter a suitable performance level and work through repertoire at increasing difficulty. | Experienced players can enter the performance chapter directly. A held pickup requires practice across the bar line, which is why isolated bars and both-bar playback serve different purposes. |

## What constitutes progress

A clean take is a learner judgment about a specific musical result. In the early release study, the listener should hear silence exactly where the score leaves a gap. In the octave study the unused string should stay silent as the high G moves from the last sixteenth to the offbeat. In the final study the two approaches must resolve on beat one in both directions. Merely reaching the end of playback does not award completion.

Duke, Simmons and Cash observed 17 advanced piano students rehearsing a short difficult passage and tested retention the next day. Practice duration and sheer repetition count did not significantly predict the performance ranking. This supports asking learners to identify and correct an audible error. The study does not establish a best bass curriculum, a universal BPM target, or a required number of repetitions. A practical recall check is to return the next day, mute the guide, play the phrase once and listen for the same checkpoint. ## Reading the evidence

Course and publisher pages establish what their authors teach. They do not compare student outcomes across courses. The ordering here is a design judgment based on those public progressions and the app’s aim of helping a player build lines against a riff. The scores, corrective drills and checkpoints are original. The song shelf opens third-party community transcriptions on the named instrument track.
